Every guest turnover should deliver a clean, safe and presentable property. A deep clean goes further by addressing tasks that cannot reasonably fit into each checkout-to-check-in window.

There is no universal interval. The right frequency depends on occupancy, guest capacity, property materials and what cleaners observe during routine turnovers.

## Understand the three cleaning layers

### Every turnover

Core reset tasks include:

- Fresh linen and towels
- Kitchen and bathroom cleaning
- Dusting accessible surfaces
- Vacuuming and mopping floors
- Waste removal
- Restocking
- Final guest-ready inspection

### Rotational detail work

One or two tasks can be added to selected turnovers:

- Wipe skirting boards in one zone
- Detail one appliance
- Vacuum beneath movable furniture
- Clean selected internal windows
- Inspect and organise one cupboard
- Dust vents or high ledges safely

### Planned deep clean

A blocked maintenance window allows broader, time-consuming work without an arriving guest creating pressure.

## Signs a deep clean is due

Do not rely only on the calendar. Look for:

- Traffic lanes or marked carpets
- Grease building around kitchen edges
- Limescale or grout discolouration
- Dust behind furniture
- Tired internal windows
- Odour that returns after routine cleaning
- Marked walls and doors
- Cluttered or disorganised stock cupboards
- Repeated guest comments about the same detail

Some signs require maintenance rather than stronger cleaning.

## Factors that shorten the interval

Deep cleaning may be needed more often with:

- High occupancy and frequent short stays
- Pets
- Family groups
- Heavy kitchen use
- Limited ventilation
- Light-coloured textiles
- Carpeted entrances
- Properties near busy roads or construction
- Long peak periods without gaps

Conversely, a lower-occupancy property with durable finishes and good rotational cleaning may need fewer full resets.

## Build a rolling maintenance calendar

Instead of writing “deep clean quarterly” without detail, assign tasks:

| Month or cycle | Planned focus |
| --- | --- |
| Cycle 1 | Kitchen appliances and cupboards |
| Cycle 2 | Carpets, upholstery and bed bases |
| Cycle 3 | Bathrooms, grout and ventilation covers |
| Cycle 4 | Internal windows, doors and high-level dusting |

Adjust the cycle from turnover reports. A heavily used oven may need attention before the rest of the kitchen.

## What to include

A holiday-let deep clean may cover:

- Detailed kitchen degreasing
- Oven and appliance interiors where agreed
- Cupboards and inventory
- Bathroom descaling and detail work
- Behind and beneath movable furniture
- Skirting boards, doors and frames
- Internal windows
- Carpet or upholstery cleaning
- Mattress and protector inspection
- Linen stock review
- High and low dusting
- Maintenance-condition report

Use our guide to [professional deep-cleaning scope](/blog/what-does-a-professional-deep-clean-include/) to build the property checklist.

## Block enough time

Do not hide a major deep clean inside an ordinary turnover price and schedule. Block the property or create a longer booking gap where necessary.

Allow drying time for carpets, upholstery, sealant or maintenance. The property should not be released until it is ready, ventilated and safe.

## Combine cleaning with inspection

A planned deep clean is a good time to inspect:

- Cookware and crockery condition
- Mattress and pillow protectors
- Linen stock
- Batteries and remotes
- Furniture fixings
- Sealant and grout
- Extractor performance
- Guest instructions
- Locked storage

Cleaning teams should report visible concerns, while qualified contractors handle electrical, gas, plumbing or other specialist work.

## Use guest feedback carefully

One comment may identify a genuine issue; a pattern is especially useful. Categorise feedback by room and task. Do not wait for a poor review if turnover reports already show deterioration.

Airbnb’s host guidance emphasises following a consistent checklist and maintaining a clean, hazard-free space for every arrival.

## Keep a record

Record:

- Date and scope
- Areas completed
- Products or specialist methods
- Stains or damage that remain
- Maintenance reported
- Inventory replaced
- Next recommended review

This prevents uncertainty across several properties and teams.
