Most cleanliness complaints are not caused by one dramatic failure. They come from small, highly visible details: hair on bedding, crumbs in a drawer, a marked glass or an unpleasant smell when the door opens.

Airbnb’s host ground rules state that listings should be clean and free of health hazards before check-in, including cleaning between stays. See Airbnb’s current [listing cleanliness requirements](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/help/article/2895).

A repeatable quality-control system reduces avoidable misses and helps hosts respond appropriately when a genuine problem occurs.

## Define the standard for each property

A generic checklist is only a starting point. Record:

- Exact bed and towel presentation
- Which cupboards guests can access
- Approved linen and consumables
- Appliance settings
- Position of remotes and information
- Areas that need a photograph
- Known maintenance limitations
- Locked owner storage

Use labelled reference photographs for presentation points. Update them when furniture, amenities or room layouts change.

## Focus on high-risk details

Pay particular attention to:

- Hair on bedding, bathroom floors and shower screens
- Food residue in ovens, microwaves and fridges
- Crumbs in drawers and beneath cushions
- Dust on bedside surfaces and headboards
- Smears on mirrors and drinking glasses
- Odour from bins, drains or damp textiles
- Marks on toilet seats and around hinges
- Used items left by a previous guest
- Incomplete toiletries or toilet-roll stock

These areas are close to the guest and easy to photograph in a complaint.

## Give the turnover enough time

A checklist cannot compensate for an impossible schedule. Set the working window using property size, bathrooms, bed changes, laundry, access and restocking—not only the gap printed on the booking calendar.

Our guide to [planning a same-day turnover](/blog/plan-a-same-day-airbnb-turnover/) explains how to calculate the real time available.

If a previous guest leaves the property in an exceptional condition, the host needs a decision process for extra help, a revised check-in or guest communication.

## Separate maintenance from cleaning

Some issues look like poor cleaning but require repair:

- Failed bathroom sealant
- Persistent drain odour
- Damp or mould from a moisture problem
- Scratched or discoloured surfaces
- Worn linen
- Damaged cookware
- Stained grout
- Faulty ventilation

Cleaners should report these concerns promptly. Repeatedly scrubbing a damaged surface will not solve the guest-facing problem.

## Use a final inspection route

After cleaning, walk through the property as a guest:

1. Approach the entrance and test access.
2. Notice the initial smell and temperature.
3. Check the main visual line through each room.
4. Inspect beds and bathroom surfaces closely.
5. Open guest-accessible cupboards and appliances.
6. Check crockery and glasses.
7. Confirm supplies.
8. Verify lights, heating and windows.
9. Remove cleaning equipment.
10. Secure the property.

The final checker should use good lighting and avoid rushing because floors are already finished.

## Use completion photographs intelligently

Photograph priority evidence:

- Made beds
- Bathroom sanitary ware
- Kitchen worktops and sink
- Fridge interior
- Main living area
- Any reported damage

Do not create hundreds of unusable images. Agree how long records are retained and handle any personal information carefully.

## Build a rapid response process

If a guest reports a problem:

- Acknowledge it promptly
- Ask for enough detail to understand the location
- Review completion records
- Offer a proportionate remedy where appropriate
- Avoid arguing before facts are known
- Record the issue and root cause

Do not ask the guest to undertake substantial cleaning themselves. Airbnb’s checkout guidance encourages keeping guest chores simple.

## Learn from repeated issues

Track complaints by:

- Property
- Room
- Task
- Cleaner or team
- Length of turnover
- Previous guest condition
- Maintenance cause
- Resolution

If the same point repeats, change the system. Add a checklist item, improve lighting, replace a worn material or allocate more time.

## Keep backup arrangements

Create cover for:

- Cleaner illness
- Delayed laundry
- Supply shortage
- Maintenance emergency
- Late guest departure
- Peak booking periods

A backup plan is part of reliable hosting, not an admission that the usual team is unreliable.
