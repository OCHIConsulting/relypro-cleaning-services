A finished checklist does not always mean a holiday let is ready for its next guest. Quality checking adds a final look at the property after the main cleaning work, when hair, streaks, missing towels and misplaced items are easier to notice.

The check should be short enough to happen on every turnover and specific enough that different people can reach a consistent decision. For hosts and managers in Derby, a repeatable arrival inspection is particularly useful when several properties share a busy checkout day.

## Begin at the entrance

Stand outside and follow the route a guest will take. Look at the threshold, entrance mat, visible floor and first view of the living area. Check that the agreed access arrangement is ready, without sharing codes in routine completion messages.

Then inspect the rooms in a fixed order. A consistent route reduces backtracking and makes it easier to remember which areas have been checked. Keep cleaning equipment out of the finished-room photographs and remove it before the final readiness decision.

## Check cleanliness and presentation separately

A bed may be neatly made but have a marked pillowcase. A clean bathroom may still be missing the agreed towels. Treat these as different checks rather than using a general impression of the room.

| Room | Cleaning check | Presentation check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bedroom | Hair, dust and visible linen marks | Correct bed setup and agreed linen |
| Bathroom | Basin, toilet, shower and floor | Towels, bath mat and supplies |
| Kitchen | Worktops, sink and appliance residue | Crockery stored and agreed stock |
| Living space | Crumbs, dust and floor edges | Furniture and guest information reset |

Check from more than one angle where light makes streaks difficult to see. Avoid disturbing freshly cleaned surfaces unnecessarily; the aim is to find omissions, not repeat the whole clean.

## Make linen checks deliberate

Inspect clean bedding before fitting it and again after making the bed. Look for visible marks, tears and an incorrect size. Check protectors when changing linen, and report anything requiring replacement.

Keep used linen separate from clean stock throughout the turnover. An attractive bed setup cannot compensate for uncertainty about whether an item is fresh. If suitable linen is unavailable, escalate the shortage before describing the room as ready.

## Record exceptions with a decision

A useful exception note says what was found, where it is and what happens next. “Bathroom needs attention” is less helpful than identifying a marked shower screen that needs additional cleaning or damaged sealant that needs maintenance.

Do not claim a repair has been made when only the surrounding area was cleaned. Agree who can authorise extra time, replacement supplies or a delayed readiness message.

Photographs should show relevant conditions and respect guest privacy. Avoid personal paperwork, reflections of people and security information. Keep image access limited to those managing the property.

## Close the loop before check-in

Decide which issues prevent readiness and which can be passed to the manager for a later maintenance visit. The cleaner should have an escalation contact when the decision exceeds the agreed scope.

Use the first few inspections to refine the checklist. If the same detail is missed repeatedly, add a specific check or reference photograph instead of relying on memory.
