A checklist makes an office cleaning agreement measurable. It tells the cleaner what to do, helps managers inspect the result and prevents low-visibility tasks from being forgotten.

The best checklist is organised by area and frequency. It should also state exclusions, consumables responsibility and any safety or access constraints.

## Entrance and reception

The entrance creates the first impression and collects outdoor soil.

**Routine tasks**

- Vacuum entrance matting
- Sweep, vacuum or mop the floor as appropriate
- Remove obvious marks from doors and internal glass
- Dust reception surfaces
- Empty bins
- Straighten agreed furniture and display items
- Wipe accessible touchpoints

**Periodic tasks**

- Deep-clean entrance matting
- Detail edges and skirting boards
- Clean internal glass fully
- Dust high ledges where safely reachable

During wet weather, the entrance may need additional checks to manage appearance and slip risks.

## Desk and workstation areas

Agree whether cleaners may move papers, personal items or equipment. A clear-desk policy makes cleaning more consistent.

**Routine tasks**

- Empty general waste and recycling
- Vacuum accessible floor areas
- Dust clear, accessible desk surfaces
- Wipe agreed shared touchpoints
- Spot-clean doors and partitions

**Periodic tasks**

- Vacuum beneath movable chairs and furniture
- Detail skirting boards and cable-area edges
- Dust accessible vents and ledges
- Clean fabric chairs or partitions if included

Electronic equipment needs appropriate methods. Do not spray products directly onto screens, keyboards or powered devices.

## Meeting rooms

Meeting rooms often have bursts of heavy use.

- Wipe clear tables
- Arrange chairs to the agreed layout
- Empty bins
- Remove cups or catering waste as agreed
- Clean whiteboards only with approved products
- Wipe door handles and shared controls
- Vacuum or mop flooring
- Spot-clean internal glass

Add a check after scheduled client days or large meetings rather than relying only on a fixed weekly routine.

## Office kitchen or tea point

Responsibility must be clear. If staff leave dishes and food, a cleaner may not have time to complete hygiene tasks.

**Routine tasks**

- Clean worktops and sink
- Wipe taps and handles
- Clean appliance exteriors
- Empty bins and replace liners
- Wipe tables
- Vacuum and mop flooring
- Replenish agreed consumables

**Rotational tasks**

- Clean microwave interior
- Wipe cupboard fronts
- Check and clean fridge shelves under the workplace policy
- Descale selected fixtures
- Detail splashbacks and edges

Set a documented process for abandoned food. Cleaners should not have to decide whether an unlabelled lunch can be discarded.

## Washrooms

Washrooms often determine how occupants judge the whole cleaning service.

- Clean and disinfect toilets and urinals
- Clean basins, taps and dispensers
- Clean mirrors
- Wipe touchpoints
- Empty sanitary and general waste under the agreed arrangement
- Replenish soap, tissue and paper products
- Spot-clean partitions and doors
- Vacuum or mop floors
- Report leaks, blockages, damage and low stock

High-occupancy workplaces may need daytime checks in addition to an evening clean.

## Corridors, stairs and lifts

- Vacuum or mop traffic routes
- Clean handrails and agreed touchpoints
- Remove marks from doors and internal glass
- Clean lift floors and mirrors
- Dust ledges and accessible signage
- Report lighting, trip or spill concerns

Confirm responsibility for external steps, shared lobbies and areas controlled by a landlord or building manager.

## Waste and recycling points

Define:

- Which bins the cleaner empties
- Where bags are taken
- How recycling streams are separated
- Who supplies liners
- How confidential waste is handled
- What happens with heavy, sharp or hazardous waste

General office cleaning should not silently absorb specialist waste responsibilities.

## Add frequencies and service standards

Beside every task, specify daily, visit-based, weekly, monthly or periodic. Avoid vague wording such as “regularly”.

Our guide to [office cleaning frequency](/blog/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned/) explains how staff numbers, footfall and building use affect the schedule.

Include a review process:

1. Named contact for routine feedback
2. Method for reporting urgent issues
3. Regular service review
4. Agreed response to missed tasks
5. Process for changing the specification
