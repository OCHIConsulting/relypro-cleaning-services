Colder, wetter weather changes how guests use a holiday let. They may bring damp shoes indoors, spend longer in living spaces and need a clear place for coats. A seasonal review helps the cleaning checklist reflect that experience.

For Derby hosts, begin with the guest's route from the door to the bedroom, bathroom and kitchen. Look for practical changes that support a clean, welcoming arrival, then separate cleaning tasks from maintenance or safety checks requiring a qualified person.

## Prepare the entrance for wet arrivals

Check the condition and position of entrance mats. Clean them according to their care instructions and arrange replacement if they are damaged or no longer usable.

Provide an obvious, uncluttered place for shoes and coats where appropriate. Avoid positioning storage where it blocks an exit or makes the entrance difficult to navigate.

Inspect the surrounding floor after each turnover and agree who responds if moisture appears between visits. A seasonal checklist is most useful when it identifies a responsible person as well as a task.

## Review soft furnishings and linen

Inspect towels, bedding and protectors for visible marks, damage and correct sizing. Any additional blankets offered to guests need a clear laundering and replacement process.

Keep clean stock dry, protected and separate from used linen. Do not add textiles to the guest setup unless the turnover process can reliably check and refresh them.

| Item | Seasonal question |
| --- | --- |
| Entrance mats | Are they clean, secure and suitable for use? |
| Towels | Is sufficient clean stock available? |
| Blankets | Is their cleaning process defined? |
| Carpets | Do marks need planned attention? |
| Storage | Can clean and used items stay separate? |

These checks support presentation and stock planning. They do not establish a fixed quantity for every property; that depends on the agreed setup and booking pattern.

## Notice moisture without masking the cause

Ask the turnover team to report visible condensation, damp patches, leaks or a persistent musty smell. Record the location and observed condition so the manager can arrange an appropriate assessment.

Do not rely on fragrance to hide an odour or repeatedly clean an area without investigating why the problem returns. Cleaning may address suitable surface residue while the source requires maintenance.

Follow property instructions for ventilation and heating settings. Cleaners should not make unapproved changes to equipment or attempt repairs outside their role.

## Schedule deeper work around bookings

If carpets, upholstery or appliance interiors need attention, allow a realistic gap and agree the method in advance. Include drying, access and any furniture movement in the plan.

Complete dusty maintenance work before the final clean where practical. The final inspection should happen after everyone who needs access has finished, so it reflects the actual arrival condition.

Confirm the next booking time before adding work to a normal turnover. An additional task is only useful if the property can still be left ready within the agreed window.

## Finish with a clear readiness message

Check the agreed room setup, supplies, linen and visible cleanliness. Report anything unresolved and identify the decision needed from the host.

Safety equipment, heating systems and other regulated or specialist checks need their own appropriate process. A cleaning completion message should not be treated as certification that those systems are safe.
