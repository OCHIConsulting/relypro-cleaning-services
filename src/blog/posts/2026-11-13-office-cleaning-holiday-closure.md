An office closure changes the cleaning routine. The last working day may finish early, a few colleagues may still come in, and the person who normally lets the cleaner in could already be on leave. A routine visit can easily become a missed visit, or a clean completed before the final round of mugs and lunch containers appears.

If your Derby workplace closes over Christmas, use November to agree a simple plan. The aim is not to add an elaborate deep clean to every contract. It is to decide what needs doing, when the space will actually be empty and who will take responsibility for the handover.

## Start with the real occupancy dates

Write down the last normal working day, any reduced-hours days and the first day staff return. Include occasional access by directors, contractors or a skeleton team. “Closed for Christmas” is not a useful instruction if people will still use the kitchen twice a week.

Then compare those dates with your usual cleaning visits. Agree which visits remain, which need rescheduling and whether any additional work is wanted. Do not assume a bank-holiday replacement, extra visit or specialist service is already included. Confirm the scope, availability and any price change before work is booked.

## Separate the final routine clean from optional extras

The final routine clean should follow your agreed specification, with particular attention to the areas used right up to closing time. An empty office can also offer a convenient opportunity for tasks that are awkward during a busy working day, but those tasks need their own agreement.

| Area | Agree before the closure |
| --- | --- |
| Desks and meeting rooms | Which surfaces staff will clear and which equipment must not be touched |
| Kitchen | Who removes food, empties cupboards where needed and authorises any appliance-interior cleaning |
| Washrooms | The final visit time and which consumables should be checked or replenished |
| Floors | Routine cleaning or separately agreed carpet or hard-floor work |
| Waste | Who handles the final bags and where they go if collections change |

Keep maintenance, confidential waste disposal and specialist work separate from routine cleaning. If something is outside the agreed service, assign it to the appropriate person rather than leaving a vague note for the cleaner.

## Give staff a short clear-down request

Send one practical internal reminder before the last working day. Ask colleagues to take personal food home, wash up their own containers and clear agreed desk surfaces. Someone should check shared cupboards and the fridge rather than assuming individual reminders will cover everything.

Do not ask cleaners to decide whether unlabelled food, papers or personal belongings can be thrown away. Name a staff contact who can make those decisions before the visit. For a wider division of responsibilities, see our guide to [who cleans what in a shared office](/blog/shared-office-cleaning-responsibilities/).

Avoid treating the cleaning visit as an IT shutdown. Staff or your IT contact should manage computers and other equipment under your own procedures. Cleaners should only work around equipment as agreed, not unplug or relocate it without permission.

## Make access work without the usual receptionist

Confirm the entry arrangement for the actual visit, not just the normal working week. Agree a named contact and backup, arrival window, authorised areas and the procedure if entry is not possible. Share access instructions through your established secure channel, not an open notice or public calendar entry.

The handover should also say who is responsible for checking doors, windows and the alarm after the visit. Cleaning completion and building security are related, but they are not automatically the same responsibility. Make the boundary explicit.

If nobody can attend an access problem, agree in advance whether the visit should be paused and how a replacement would be arranged. This is more useful than expecting a cleaner to improvise outside a locked building.

## Use a final-day checklist

Keep the list short enough that someone will actually complete it:

- Confirm the last occupants have finished using the areas to be cleaned.
- Remove personal food and identify anything that must stay.
- Clear the agreed surfaces without leaving confidential papers exposed.
- Confirm waste arrangements and access to the correct bins.
- Check that the cleaner has the approved entry and exit instructions.
- Record any excluded rooms or unfinished maintenance work.
- Confirm how completion and any outstanding issue will be reported.

For more detailed task boundaries, our [cleaning scope-of-work guide](/blog/cleaning-scope-of-work-template/) can help turn a general request into a usable specification.

## Plan the first morning back as well

Decide whether the final clean is enough or whether a visit shortly before reopening would be useful. This depends on the closure length, any work carried out while staff are away and the service you have agreed. A building that hosted contractors during the break may need a different plan from one that stayed unused.

Ask a named colleague to check the kitchen, washrooms, waste and entrance before normal occupancy resumes. Report maintenance issues through your building process; a cleaning visit is not a substitute for technical checks.

## Put the agreement in one place

One short handover should capture dates, tasks, exclusions, access, contacts and the return-to-work plan. Share the final version with the people who need it, and update it if the closure dates change.
