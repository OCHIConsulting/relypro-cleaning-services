Not every change of tenant requires carpet extraction, but every turnaround should include a careful carpet inspection. The decision depends on condition, material, odour, stains, occupancy and the standard recorded at the start of the tenancy.

## Inspect before other work changes the evidence

During checkout, record:

- General soil and traffic lanes
- Individual stains
- Burns, pulls or tears
- Flattening and wear
- Odour
- Pet hair or contamination
- Furniture marks
- Condition at edges and thresholds

Compare this with the check-in inventory. Distinguish removable soil from wear or damage, as cleaning cannot restore every change.

## Start with thorough vacuuming

An apparently dull carpet may improve with slow, methodical vacuuming:

- Remove loose items
- Use the appropriate machine setting
- Vacuum in overlapping passes
- Detail accessible edges
- Move only safe, light furniture
- Check corners and behind doors

Vacuuming should happen before deciding whether spots or the full area need wet treatment.

## Use spot treatment for isolated marks

If the carpet is otherwise clean, targeted treatment may be sufficient. Results depend on the fibre, spill and time elapsed.

Record any product already used and test treatments in an inconspicuous area. Our [carpet spill first-response guide](/blog/what-to-do-when-you-spill-on-carpet/) explains why blotting and controlled moisture are safer than aggressive scrubbing.

No responsible technician should guarantee every stain will disappear.

## Consider extraction cleaning when

Professional extraction may be appropriate if:

- Traffic lanes remain visibly soiled
- Several stains affect the room
- Odour remains after dry cleaning steps
- The carpet has not been deeply cleaned for a long period
- Pet occupation requires more thorough treatment
- The incoming inventory needs a clearly reset standard
- Manufacturer care guidance supports the method

The technician should identify the fibre where possible, assess colourfastness and select a compatible process.

## Allow sufficient drying time

Do not schedule wet carpet cleaning immediately before photography, inventory or tenant arrival.

Drying time varies with:

- Carpet and backing construction
- Amount of moisture used
- Ventilation
- Indoor temperature and humidity
- Air movement
- Level of soil and repeated treatment

Keep foot traffic low, ventilate safely and avoid placing furniture onto damp carpet. Metal or wood furniture can transfer marks.

## Cleaning versus replacement

Cleaning is unlikely to correct:

- Worn or missing pile
- Burns
- Bleaching or dye loss
- Large tears
- Delamination
- Persistent contamination in the underlay
- Strong odour from an unresolved source

Request an honest assessment before spending repeatedly on a carpet that requires repair or replacement.

## Coordinate carpet work with the turnaround

Use this sequence:

1. Checkout inspection and evidence
2. Waste removal
3. Repairs and decorating
4. General property clean
5. Carpet treatment
6. Drying and ventilation
7. Final inspection and inventory

Extraction after decorating removes some construction dust and avoids painters walking across a finished carpet.

## Be precise about deposit issues

Landlords and agents need evidence for proposed deductions, and tenants should not be charged automatically for a professional clean. Current government guidance states that a property may be expected to be returned to an appropriate standard, but mandatory cleaning fees are restricted.

Cleaning invoices show work completed; they do not by themselves prove who caused a condition or whether a deduction is fair. Keep check-in and checkout evidence.

## Ask for a useful carpet-cleaning quote

Provide:

- Number and approximate size of rooms
- Stairs, landings and rugs
- Known carpet fibre
- Photographs of stains
- Pet or odour information
- Parking and access
- Water and power availability
- Required completion date
