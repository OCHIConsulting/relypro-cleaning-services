A same-day turnover is a timed operational process. The work begins when the previous guest leaves and ends only when the property has been inspected, restocked and secured for the next arrival.

Trying to save time by shortening the cleaning window can increase the risk of missed details. A better approach is to remove uncertainty before checkout day.

## Calculate the true working window

Start with:

- Published checkout time
- Likely time the cleaner can enter
- Published check-in time
- Buffer for final inspection
- Travel between properties
- Building or key-access restrictions

The apparent five-hour gap may provide far less cleaning time if guests leave late, lifts are busy or linen must be collected elsewhere.

Airbnb’s guidance for busy periods recommends checklists, extra linen and a backup support plan. Its [busy-season hosting advice](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/resources/hosting-homes/a/simplifying-hosting-during-the-busy-season-610) is a useful reminder that simpler checkout instructions can also reduce friction.

## Set a property-specific duration

Estimate time using:

- Bedrooms and bathrooms
- Bed configurations
- Floor area
- Kitchen use
- Laundry arrangement
- Stairs and access
- Guest capacity
- Restocking requirements
- Condition variation between stays

Do not price or schedule every two-bedroom apartment identically. Two bathrooms, a sofa bed or on-site laundry can materially change the work.

## Prepare before the guest checks out

At least a day before:

- Confirm the booking has no extension
- Verify cleaner access
- Share any guest-reported issue
- Confirm linen availability
- Check consumable stock
- Review maintenance work
- Identify early check-in requests
- Confirm who can authorise additional work

Avoid promising an early arrival until the turnover team confirms completion.

## Use a clear arrival routine

On entry, the cleaner should:

1. Confirm the property is vacant.
2. Check for urgent damage, hazards or excessive waste.
3. Photograph and report significant concerns.
4. Open curtains and ventilate where appropriate.
5. Start linen processing immediately if it is on-site.
6. Collect rubbish and guest belongings.
7. Work through the room checklist.

If the property condition is far outside the normal scope, the host needs a quick decision process for extra time, support or delayed check-in.

## Sequence the cleaning efficiently

A common sequence is:

1. Strip beds and start laundry.
2. Apply suitable products where dwell time is required.
3. Clean kitchen and bathrooms.
4. Dust bedrooms and living spaces.
5. Make beds with inspected linen.
6. Restock agreed items.
7. Vacuum and mop floors last.
8. Complete final presentation and security checks.

Teams should avoid crossing over each other or placing clean items in unfinished rooms.

## Separate turnover and deep-clean tasks

Not every periodic task belongs in every same-day clean. Create a rotating plan for:

- Oven interiors
- Detailed grout work
- Behind large furniture
- High-level dusting
- Carpet extraction
- Internal windows
- Cupboard inventories

Scheduling one or two rotational tasks during suitable stays prevents build-up without overloading the turnover window. For larger work, block a maintenance day.

## Build linen and stock resilience

Keep enough complete linen sets to absorb delayed laundry and rejected items. Our [linen management guide](/blog/airbnb-linen-management-guide/) explains how to track usable sets.

For consumables, define minimum quantities and a reorder point. A cleaner cannot solve a stock shortage during the last 20 minutes unless purchasing authority and nearby supply options are already agreed.

## Create a final quality-control check

The person completing the check should follow the guest journey:

- Entrance is clean and access works
- Property has no strong or unpleasant odour
- Beds and towels match the reference presentation
- No visible hair remains in bathrooms or bedding
- Kitchenware is clean and put away
- Bins are empty
- Supplies meet the agreed level
- Heating, lights and appliances are left correctly
- Windows and doors are secure
- Guest information and remotes are in position

Completion photographs can document priority areas, but they should be targeted and quick.

## Plan for late departures and emergencies

Write down what happens if:

- A guest has not left
- The property needs excessive cleaning
- A cleaner is unavailable
- Linen delivery is late
- Water or electricity fails
- Maintenance prevents safe occupation
- The next guest requests early check-in

Include backup contacts and define who communicates with the guest. The cleaner should not be left to make booking or refund decisions.

## Measure and improve

Record actual arrival, completion and issue times for several turnovers. If one property repeatedly overruns, adjust the checklist, staffing, checkout time or booking gap.
