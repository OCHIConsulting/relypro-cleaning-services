A cleaning scope of work turns “keep the property clean” into a set of tasks that someone can plan, price and check. It helps households, landlords and businesses understand what is included and where another arrangement is needed.

The document does not need to be complicated. It does need to describe the rooms, priorities, frequency and practical conditions of the work. A clear one-page brief can prevent more confusion than several pages of vague service descriptions.

## Describe the property and its use

Start with the location, type of property and number of rooms. For an office, include occupancy and working hours. For a rental, describe the bed setup, bathrooms and expected changeover pattern.

List floor types and any surfaces needing specific care. Note restrictions such as rooms that remain occupied or cupboards that must not be opened. Include access and parking arrangements that affect the available cleaning time.

Keep entry codes and other security details in an appropriate restricted process rather than a broadly distributed quotation document.

## Write tasks people can verify

“Kitchen cleaned” can mean several different things. “Clean accessible worktops, sink, splashback and appliance exteriors” provides a more usable starting point.

Separate routine work from periodic tasks and work available only by agreement. This keeps occasional appliance interiors or carpet extraction from becoming an assumption within every visit.

| Area | Task | Frequency or trigger | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kitchen | Worktops and sink | Agreed visits | Clear surfaces required |
| Floors | Vacuum and mop as suitable | Agreed visits | Accessible areas |
| Appliances | Specified interior clean | Separately agreed | Preparation required |
| Supplies | Check agreed stock | Where included | Purchase authority defined |

The table is a template, not a universal RelyPro package. Replace each example with the work agreed for your property.

## Clarify supplies and preparation

State who provides products, equipment, bin liners and consumables. If a site requires particular products, share the requirements before the company quotes.

Explain what the client needs to do before arrival. Clearing personal items, emptying an appliance or arranging safe access may be necessary for a particular task. A missed preparation step should have an agreed response.

The cleaning method must suit the surface and product instructions. Do not specify an outcome that would require unsafe access or treatment unsuitable for the material.

## Define exceptions and extra work

Agree how the cleaner reports tasks blocked by clutter, access problems or unexpected conditions. Name the contact who can approve extra time or a revised scope.

Record exclusions clearly: repairs, hazardous waste, inaccessible high areas or substantial removal work should not be assumed to sit within a standard clean. Where specialist help is needed, identify that need before scheduling.

Include a process for changes in occupancy, property condition or room use. A scope agreed for a quiet office may need review after expansion.

## Decide how completion is checked

Choose a proportionate method: a completed checklist, exception note or agreed photographs. Define what happens when something needs attention, including whom to contact and how the concern will be assessed.

Review the scope after the first few visits. Practical feedback can reveal a task that takes longer than expected or an instruction that is too open to interpretation.
