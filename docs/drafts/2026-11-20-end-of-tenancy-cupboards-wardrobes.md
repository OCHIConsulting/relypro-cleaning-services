An empty room can still contain a surprisingly full cupboard. When moving day gets busy, wardrobe shelves, bathroom cabinets and the space under the sink can become the last places anyone checks. If you are arranging end-of-tenancy cleaning, these small storage areas deserve a place in the plan, not a last-minute assumption.

The useful question is not simply whether cupboards will be cleaned. It is which cupboards, which surfaces, when they will be empty and what happens to anything left inside. Agree those details before your Derby move-out clean so the work can be scoped realistically.

## Make a storage list before you pack

Walk through the property and note the storage that belongs to each room. Include built-in wardrobes, kitchen units, bathroom cabinets, airing cupboards and any freestanding furniture supplied with the property. A cupboard above a doorway is easy to forget when most of your packing is happening at floor level.

Separate your own furniture from items staying at the property. If your wardrobe is leaving with the removal van, the cleaning discussion may concern the floor it leaves behind. If it belongs to the property, its shelves, drawers and external surfaces may need to be included in the agreed specification.

This is also a useful time to flag unusual finishes, damaged hinges or loose shelves. Describe what you can see without trying to diagnose the cause. Cleaning and repair should remain separate decisions.

## Agree interiors and exteriors explicitly

“Clean the bedroom” does not explain whether a wardrobe interior is included. Likewise, wiping a cupboard door is different from emptying the unit and cleaning every shelf. Ask for the scope to distinguish these tasks.

| Storage area | Clarify before the visit |
| --- | --- |
| Kitchen cupboards | Doors, handles, empty shelves and accessible internal surfaces |
| Built-in wardrobes | Shelves, hanging space, drawers and any mirrors |
| Bathroom cabinets | Empty interiors, external finish and mirrored doors |
| Utility storage | What can be accessed without moving equipment or disturbing connections |
| Supplied furniture | Which items stay and which surfaces are included |

Do not assume cleaners will dismantle units, lift heavy furniture or remove fitted panels. If access requires that kind of work, discuss it beforehand and agree who will handle it. ## Empty cupboards before the cleaning starts

Give yourself a clear deadline for removing belongings. Ideally, the areas included in the clean will be empty before the cleaner arrives, rather than gradually becoming available while the visit is under way.

Use three simple groups: belongings to take, items that stay with the property and anything whose ownership needs checking. Ask the relevant person about uncertain items before the visit. Do not leave the cleaner to decide whether an unlabelled bottle, spare fitting or box of kitchenware should be discarded.

If some storage must remain in use until departure, identify it and agree the sequence. A final essentials box can be easier to manage than reopening several cupboards after they have been cleaned. In winter, allow for the practical disruption of coats, shoes and wet moving boxes entering the property after the main packing is finished.

## Keep cleaning separate from disposal and maintenance

Removing belongings, clearing unwanted items and cleaning empty surfaces are different jobs. Confirm waste arrangements yourself and ask whether any agreed removal is included, rather than assuming a cleaning quote covers it.

Point out leaks, damaged surfaces, loose fittings or an unusual patch inside a cabinet. Do not cover them up or expect routine cleaning to resolve the underlying issue. Report them through the appropriate property contact and agree whether the area should be left untouched pending advice.

For delicate or unfamiliar materials, share any available care instructions. Avoid applying a new product just before the cleaner arrives in the hope of speeding things up. The appropriate method depends on the surface and the provider's assessment, not a universal household shortcut.

## Use a short final check

Before handing the property over, check the storage areas against the scope you agreed:

- Open each included cupboard and drawer to check nothing personal remains.
- Confirm items belonging to the property are in their agreed places.
- Check the accessible shelves, doors and handles included in the clean.
- Note any excluded area or outstanding maintenance issue.
- Avoid putting packing materials back into cleaned storage.
- Keep the agreed completion record and raise questions promptly.

This is a practical handover check, not a promise about an inspection outcome or a deposit. The cleaning specification should make clear what has been requested and completed.
