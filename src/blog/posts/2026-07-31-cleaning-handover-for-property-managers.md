A cleaning partner can only work confidently when the property instructions are clear. For a Derby property manager taking on a new apartment or holiday let, the handover is where a repeatable service begins. Missing access details, unclear linen arrangements or an uncertain finish time can undermine an otherwise well-planned clean.

Use the first visit to agree how the property should be left, what the cleaner is responsible for and who decides when something unexpected happens. A short, current property guide is more useful than a long message thread containing several versions of the instructions.

## Start with a property walkthrough

Walk through the property in the same order a guest would experience it. Record the entrance, bedrooms, bathrooms, kitchen and living spaces, including any awkward areas that need extra time. Count actual beds and linen changes rather than relying on the advertised bedroom count.

Explain which cupboards can be opened, which belongings must remain untouched and where waste should be taken. Identify fragile finishes and provide appliance instructions where they affect cleaning. Do not assume every surface can tolerate the same product.

Photographs of the agreed room layout can help explain presentation standards. Keep security-sensitive instructions in a restricted channel rather than in photographs or documents shared widely.

## Agree the scope before the first booking

Separate routine turnover work from occasional tasks. An oven interior, carpet extraction or substantial limescale removal may need different equipment, extra time or a separate quotation.

| Area | Handover decision |
| --- | --- |
| Beds | Linen supplied, sizes, spare sets and presentation |
| Kitchen | Appliance checks and agreed interior cleaning |
| Supplies | Minimum stock levels and who buys replacements |
| Reporting | What to report, to whom and by when |
| Completion | Checklist, final checks and escalation contact |

Use this table as a discussion aid, then write the property-specific answers into the working checklist. If a task is excluded, record who will handle it so it does not disappear between suppliers.

## Make access dependable

Confirm the arrival window, parking restrictions and a backup contact. Test the agreed access method before a time-sensitive changeover. Share codes securely and update the instructions whenever access arrangements change.

A cleaner who cannot enter should know how long to wait and whom to call. Equally, agree what happens if a guest has not left. Entry and checkout disputes belong with the property manager; cleaners should not be expected to resolve them alone.

## Set a simple reporting routine

Agree a short completion message covering finished work, exceptions and any decision still required. Report damage as an observation, with a location and appropriate photograph, without guessing who caused it.

For stock shortages, state the item and quantity remaining. For a maintenance issue, explain its effect on readiness. A slow drain and an unusable shower require different responses, even if both appear on the same checklist.

Avoid including guest documents, faces, entry codes or personal belongings in routine completion photographs. Share only what the manager needs to assess the issue.

## Review after the first few turnovers

Compare the planned cleaning window with what the property actually requires. Repeated overruns may point to insufficient linen, difficult parking or an unrealistic task list. Adjust the arrangement before the next busy period.
