Washrooms can make an otherwise well-maintained office feel poorly managed. A once-daily clean may be adequate for a lightly occupied site, while a busy workplace needs checks throughout the day.

The schedule should respond to actual use and define both cleaning tasks and supply responsibilities.

## Measure demand

Record:

- Average daily occupancy
- Peak attendance days
- Visitor numbers
- Number of washrooms and fixtures
- Opening hours
- Events or shift changes
- Existing complaint patterns
- Frequency of supply shortages

Hybrid offices may need more attention Tuesday to Thursday than on quieter days. Review use rather than applying the same schedule automatically.

## Separate full cleans and service checks

A **full clean** normally covers all fixtures, surfaces, floors and waste.

A **service check** is a shorter visit to:

- Inspect the condition
- Remove visible contamination
- Replenish supplies
- Empty bins where required
- Spot-clean mirrors and touchpoints
- Address floor spills
- Report blockages, leaks or damage

This distinction makes pricing and expectations clearer.

## Full washroom checklist

Subject to the site specification:

- Clean and disinfect toilets and urinals
- Clean seats, hinges, flush points and exteriors
- Clean basins, taps and splash areas
- Clean mirrors
- Wipe dispensers and touchpoints
- Spot-clean partitions, doors and walls
- Empty general and agreed sanitary waste
- Replenish soap, tissue and paper products
- Vacuum and mop floors
- Clean floor edges on rotation
- Report plumbing or ventilation concerns

Products need suitable contact time and must be used according to the label.

## Decide how often checks are needed

Use observed condition as your guide:

- **Low occupancy:** one thorough clean per working day may be sufficient.
- **Moderate occupancy:** full clean plus a scheduled daytime check.
- **High footfall or public access:** multiple checks during opening hours.
- **Events or shift changes:** checks immediately before or after peaks.

These are starting points. Record when standards fall and adjust timing.

## Control consumables

Define:

- Approved soap, tissue and paper products
- Minimum stock held on site
- Reorder responsibility
- Delivery location
- Dispenser compatibility
- Emergency stock
- Reporting method

Running out of soap or toilet tissue is often a stock-control failure rather than a cleaning failure.

## Handle waste clearly

The contract should identify:

- Bin types covered
- Liner supply
- Waste destination
- Sanitary-waste provider
- Sharps or hazardous-waste escalation
- Spill response

Ordinary office cleaners should not handle unknown sharps or specialist waste without the correct system, training and equipment.

## Include maintenance reporting

Cleaners may notice:

- Leaking taps or cisterns
- Slow drains
- Loose seats or dispensers
- Damaged sealant
- Failed lighting
- Poor ventilation
- Recurring odour

Provide a named contact and expected response. Cleaning cannot solve an odour caused by a plumbing or drainage fault.

## Protect privacy and access

Use signs and a safe process for temporarily closing a washroom. Consider:

- Occupied-building working times
- Accessible toilet availability
- Lone-working arrangements
- Gender and privacy considerations
- Emergency access
- Floor-drying time

Avoid cleaning all available facilities simultaneously unless suitable alternatives remain.

## Review performance

Use:

- Dated service records
- Periodic manager inspections
- Supply usage
- Complaint themes
- Maintenance response times
- Cleaner feedback
