The gap between tenants is often short, but cleaning is only one part of making a rental property ready. Keys, inventories, safety checks, repairs, meter readings and contractor access all need to happen in the correct order.

A written turnaround plan reduces idle days and prevents one contractor undoing another’s work.

## Confirm possession and access

Before scheduling work, establish:

- The confirmed checkout date and key-return process
- Whether all occupants have vacated
- Who can authorise contractor access
- Alarm, entry and parking instructions
- Any building-management restrictions
- The target date for marketing, viewings or move-in

Do not assume a cleaner can enter simply because the tenancy has ended. Provide lawful, confirmed access and a named contact for problems.

## Complete the checkout inspection first

Record the property’s condition before cleaning or repairs alter the evidence. Compare it with the check-in inventory and note:

- Cleanliness by room
- Damage beyond apparent wear
- Missing items or keys
- Abandoned possessions and waste
- Appliance condition
- Maintenance concerns
- Meter readings where appropriate
- Garden, balcony or storage condition

Use clear photographs and written notes. Cleaning should not be used to conceal damage, damp or a maintenance issue that needs investigation.

## Separate the work into four lists

Organise findings so the right person receives them:

1. **Cleaning:** removable dirt, grease, limescale, dust and waste.
2. **Maintenance:** leaks, loose fittings, failed sealant, damaged appliances or ventilation problems.
3. **Decoration:** paint damage, wall preparation and finishing work.
4. **Safety and compliance:** checks that must be completed by appropriately qualified people.

This prevents a cleaning team losing time on jobs outside its scope and makes quotations easier to compare.

## Choose the right work sequence

A practical order is:

1. Remove authorised waste and remaining contents.
2. Complete intrusive repairs and messy maintenance.
3. Allow decorating, sealants or treatments to dry.
4. Carry out the detailed clean.
5. Complete specialist floor or carpet cleaning.
6. Inspect and correct any final points.
7. Record the move-in condition and inventory.

If cleaners attend before drilling, painting or appliance replacement, the property will need another clean.

## Cleaning scope for a rental reset

Provide a room list and identify whether the clean should cover:

- Kitchen cupboards inside and out
- Oven, hob, extractor and appliances
- Bathrooms, tiles, screens and limescale
- Doors, frames, skirting boards and radiators
- Internal windows and tracks
- Wardrobes and storage
- Carpets, edges and hard floors
- Balconies or communal entrance areas
- Rubbish removal

Clarify optional services such as oven cleaning and carpet extraction. Our detailed [end-of-tenancy checklist](/blog/end-of-tenancy-cleaning-checklist-derby/) explains the areas commonly reviewed during a handover.

## Build in time for drying and reinspection

Fresh sealant, paint and extracted carpets need time to dry. Do not plan the final inventory or new tenant arrival immediately after wet work.

Reserve a small correction window after the clean. A daylight inspection may identify a cupboard, mark or maintenance issue that was not visible earlier.

## Restock only what belongs with the property

Before move-in, confirm:

- Keys and fobs are complete and labelled
- Appliance manuals are available
- Bins have appropriate liners
- Smoke and carbon-monoxide alarm responsibilities are addressed
- Agreed furnishings are present
- Previous tenants’ food and toiletries are removed
- Welcome or information packs are current

Cleaning staff can report visible concerns, but safety and legal compliance remain the landlord or managing agent’s responsibility.

## Create the new evidence pack

Once all work is complete:

- Take dated photographs of each room
- Photograph appliance interiors and sanitary fittings
- Update the inventory
- Record meter readings as appropriate
- Retain contractor invoices
- Note any accepted marks or limitations
- Give the tenant the opportunity to review the inventory

Accurate records protect both parties and make the next checkout more straightforward.

## Improve the process after every tenancy

Track which problems recur. Frequent wall marks may suggest better entrance matting or more durable paint. Repeated limescale could justify a maintenance or cleaning adjustment. Missing keys may require a better handover procedure.

A short post-turnaround review can reduce cost and delay on the next property.
